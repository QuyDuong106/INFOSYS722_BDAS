\subsection{Broader and More Efficient Tuning}
Future experiments will expand training-only optimisation beyond the current finite budgets. Increasing the Optuna budget should be accompanied by convergence diagnostics, repeated sampler seeds and recorded runtime, rather than assuming more trials necessarily improve held-out accuracy. Conditional search spaces can examine tree counts, depth/leaf controls, sampling and regularisation more systematically. Pruning, successive halving, early stopping against inner validation data and resource-aware scheduling can reduce unpromising fits. Every early-stopping decision must use inner validation records, never the outer test set.

A further cycle can compare selector tree depth, elimination step, retained feature count and scaling inside the search, rather than optimising classifier settings while fixing preparation controls. Stacking experiments can compare base-learner subsets, out-of-fold fold counts, regularised meta-learners and calibration, with every nested fit isolated from outer test records. Multi-objective searches could examine discrimination, recall, false-positive burden and runtime. They would require a predefined selection rule; reporting the most favourable test metric after searching several objectives would not be a fair comparison.

The native Spark CART20/RFE20/RFE10 runs should be completed and archived with full per-candidate CV traces, selected fields and test predictions. This would close the baseline's missing inner-CV evidence gap. CUDA XGBoost and other compatible accelerators can be benchmarked against CPU execution under the same protocol; native Spark classifiers should not be described as GPU models merely because Colab provides a GPU. Caching reusable training partitions and resuming completed trials can improve workflow efficiency, but speedup must be measured for this small cohort.

\subsection{Additional Candidate Models and Selection Methods}
Further candidate families include support-vector machines, distance-based KNN, Naive Bayes and small regularised neural networks, with model-specific scaling and tuning. Elastic-net logistic regression, calibrated linear models, alternative boosting implementations and soft voting could provide simpler or complementary ensembles. Embedded sparsity, permutation-based selection and stability selection can be compared with CART/RFE while fitting selection inside validation folds. These are proposed comparisons, not algorithms already empirically ranked by this submission.

\subsection{Additional Validation and Resampling Strategies}
The next validation cycle can compare repeated nested stratified $k$-fold CV, the prepared nested LOPO workflow and bootstrap-based uncertainty assessment. Any bootstrap or resampling-based evaluation must keep assessment observations outside model and tuning fits and account for the selection procedure. Temporal validation or leave-site-out/grouped validation would be more relevant to deployment if suitable dates, centres or patient identifiers become available. Repeating random seeds alone does not reproduce these distribution shifts.

\paragraph{Jackknifing.} Jackknife resampling is a further strategy not yet applied. It repeatedly omits one observation and recomputes a statistic, such as a coefficient, a calibration summary or a selected-feature frequency, to study sensitivity and estimate bias/variance where its assumptions are suitable. It shares the leave-one-out construction with LOPO but has a different primary purpose: LOPO assesses excluded-patient predictions, whereas jackknifing studies variation in a chosen statistic. It must not be presented as an independent validation cohort or as proof that classification performance improves. Nonsmooth statistics and discontinuous feature selection require particular care; the \href{https://www.stat.cmu.edu/~cshalizi/490/23/2023-11-29-jackknife.pdf}{jackknife notes} explain its role in bias and variance assessment.

\subsection{Calibration, External Validation and Reproducibility}
Probability calibration and recall-oriented threshold selection should be defined within training before external evaluation. Subgroup analysis, decision-curve or cost-sensitive evaluation, and testing on a genuinely external clinical cohort would assess whether internal gains translate to useful decisions. The current overlapping holdouts and repeated LOPO seeds do not establish that evidence.

Future runs will retain exact package/runtime versions, immutable source references, raw-data/code signatures, complete tuning traces and locked evaluation plans. The GitHub repository and shared Google Drive folder provide the current distribution and persistence mechanism. Deployment should follow external validation and appropriate clinical review.
